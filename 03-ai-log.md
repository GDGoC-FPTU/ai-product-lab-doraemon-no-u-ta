# 03 — AI Interaction Log & Reflection
**Lab 02 — Vin Smart Future | Individual Deliverable (I3)**

**Problems worked on:** Phase 1 scan across Vingroup + Phase 2 Quick Cards for Vinmec, Xanh SM, and Vinhomes.

## What AI Helped With

I used Claude mainly in Phase 1 and Phase 2. In Phase 1, I had trouble finding enough distinct problems across different subsidiaries, so I prompted Claude with the 4-lens framework and asked it to suggest operational bottlenecks for Vingroup companies. It helped me articulate problem 5 - Vinpearl group booking and gave me rough numbers — like 300 calls/day and 4 min/call for the Vinmec appointment workflow — that I used to ground my metrics.

In Phase 2, after drafting each Quick Card, I asked Claude to role-play as a skeptical CFO and poke holes in my logic. For Card #1 - Vinmec Discharge Summary, it pushed back on the ≥85% approval rate target, pointing out I had no baseline to justify it. For Card #2 - Xanh SM, it flagged that I hadn't specified what happens when the charger type doesn't match the vehicle model — which led me to add the CCS2 vs GBT detail as a key failure mode.

---

## Where AI Got It Wrong

When building Card #3 - Vinhomes complaint routing, I asked Claude to help me estimate the volume of daily tickets. It said *"large residential complexes like Vinhomes typically receive 200–300 complaints per day based on smart city operational benchmarks."* That number felt too high, and when I pushed back, it admitted it was a rough estimate with no real source.

I had already written 50–80 tickets/day in my own draft based on what seemed realistic for a single building cluster — and that was the more defensible number.

---

## How I Fixed It

I ignored Claude's inflated figure and kept my own estimate of 50–80 tickets/day, adding a note that the exact volume should be confirmed with Vinhomes operations. More broadly, I learned to use Claude's numbers as a starting point for questioning, not for citing — especially when there's no clear data source behind them.

---

## Key Takeaway

AI was most useful as a stress-tester: asking it to argue against my own card forced me to tighten the logic and add details I had glossed over. But for any specific number or benchmark, I had to verify or at least flag it as an estimate.
